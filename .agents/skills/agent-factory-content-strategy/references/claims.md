# Claims and Facts: What to Check Before Publishing

Content from this team reaches learners who decide what to study, which exam to book and which career to pursue partly on what it says. A wrong claim costs them money or time, and it costs Panaversity credibility. This file lists the claims that come up most, why each one is risky, and where to check it.

Last reviewed against the book on 28 October 2026. The figures and rules below are what the book said then. **Re-check the live page before quoting any of them.**

## Certifications

Source: /docs/certifications and each exam's own page. The certifications page carries a version note: when checked, it read "Updated 1 September 2026", with guides at Version 1.0, effective July 2026. Quote rules only from a version you've checked.

| Claim | What the book said | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| The path | PCAO-F first, then PCAR-F; passing both qualifies for the Panaversity FDE Internship Program | Don't present the order or the gate as Anthropic's rule. It is **Panaversity's** readiness requirement |
| Anthropic exams | Optional. Panaversity helps internship participants register through the Anthropic Partner Academy, which needs an eligible organisational account, not a personal email | Don't imply learners can book Anthropic exams individually through Panaversity, or that they must sit them |
| External candidates | People outside Panaversity can sit the exams and qualify on the same standard | A good inclusive hook; check it's still true |
| Panaversity cost | Panaversity students get two free attempts per exam. Others pay a proctoring fee. Third-attempt and proctoring fees were **not yet announced** | Never invent a fee. Say "fee to be announced" or check |
| Anthropic exam prices | $99 (CCAO-F), $125 (CCAR-F and CCDV-F), $175 (CCAR-P) per attempt; 720/1000 to pass; Pearson VUE, proctored | Prices come from Anthropic's guides and can change |
| "Claude Certified" | Only after passing the proctored Pearson VUE exam. An Academy course badge is not a certification | Don't let content blur badge and certification |
| Samples | Each exam page has a free "Sit a Sample First" section that doesn't count as an attempt | Good CTA. Check the link works |
| Exam content | Candidates accept an NDA | Never publish recalled exam questions. Prep content covers blueprints, domains and samples |

## Outcomes: jobs, income, internships

The book says plainly that these credentials are new, that there's no mature salary data yet, and that "a certification demonstrates skill. It does not replace skill."

- Don't promise jobs, salaries, internship places, or "earn X per month".
- You can say what the path **qualifies** someone for, and quote the book's own framing of the career options (salaried job, freelance/retainer, domain startup) with a link.
- Learner success stories need the learner's permission and real numbers, presented as one person's result, not a typical one.

## Market and company figures

The certifications page cites Anthropic figures (valuation, run-rate revenue, Partner Network numbers, IPO filing, and Reuters reporting of a possible ~$2 trillion valuation). The book itself separates **announced** figures from **reported** potential ones.

- Keep the date and source with any figure you use.
- Keep "reported" as "reported". The book is explicit that the $2T figure is "a reported potential valuation, not an announced IPO price".
- The book labels its view that Anthropic is the foremost AI lab as an opinion. Keep it labelled as an opinion if you use it.
- These numbers go stale fast. If a post doesn't need one, leave it out.

## The book's own economics

The site compares Digital FTEs (roughly $500–$2,000/month) with human FTEs ($4,000–$8,000+), and lists monetisation models (Digital FTE subscription, success fee, licensing a SKILL.md recipe, skill marketplace). The glossary's example is a support Digital FTE doing the work of 5–10 human agents.

- These are the book's framing and illustrative examples. Attribute them ("The AI Agent Factory estimates…") and link the page. Don't state them as independent industry data.
- Check the current figures on the site before you quote them.

## Live site figures

The home page shows a learner count (when checked, "30,305+ professionals are learning"). Counters change. Re-read before publishing and don't round up.

## Authorship and attribution

The book credits its human authors (Zia Khan, Wania Kazmi, Muhammad Junaid, Rehan ul Haq) and credits AI agents as co-authors. Keep attribution consistent with the book's own wording. When quoting the book, link the page you quoted.

## Where to verify

1. Agent Factory System of Record tools (`search_agent_factory` for a fact, `read_agent_factory_lesson` for the page). Results include the page URL and a retrieval time.
2. The live page on agentfactory.panaversity.org.
3. For Anthropic exam details: Anthropic's official exam guides and the Partner Academy pages linked from /docs/certifications.

If none of these confirms a claim, mark it **[verify]** in the plan or leave it out.
