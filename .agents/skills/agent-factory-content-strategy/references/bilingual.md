# Bilingual Playbook: English, Roman Urdu, Urdu

The team serves two audiences at once: a global English-reading audience, and Pakistani learners (many from the PIAIC community) who read English for technical material but think, scroll and talk in Urdu or Roman Urdu. Treat these as different audiences with different habits, not one audience with a translation step.

The guidance below is practical rather than measured. Where the team has its own analytics (which videos or posts in which language perform), those beat this file.

## Choosing the language for a piece

| Situation | Default language | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Developer tutorial, code walkthrough | English | Code, docs, error messages and search queries are in English |
| LinkedIn thought leadership, executive content | English | Professional register; the audience is international |
| Searchable article meant to rank globally | English | Search demand for technical topics is overwhelmingly in English |
| Instagram / Facebook caption for Pakistani learners | Roman Urdu (with English terms) | How the audience actually types; fast to read on a phone |
| YouTube title and thumbnail text for a Pakistan-facing video | Roman Urdu or mixed | Matches how people search YouTube; Nastaliq on thumbnails is hard to read at small sizes |
| Long YouTube explainer for beginners | Spoken Urdu, with English terms; title in Roman Urdu or English | Beginners learn concepts faster in their first language |
| Video subtitles | Urdu script and/or English | Accessibility; Urdu script reads well at subtitle size |
| Formal announcement (exam window, cohort launch) | English and Urdu script | Formality plus reach; both audiences need the exact facts |
| WhatsApp / community message | Roman Urdu | The channel's native register |

When a channel serves both audiences (for example the main Panaversity YouTube), keep a consistent rule per **series**, so that subscribers know what they're getting. For example, "the Crash Course Companion series is in Urdu; the Builder Spotlight series is in English".

## Terminology rules

Keep these in English in every language, written exactly as the book writes them:

- Book concepts: Agent Factory, Digital FTE, AI-Native Company, Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), Vertical FDE, System of Record, Mode 1 / Mode 2, harness, loop, spec-driven development
- Product and tool names: Claude Code, OpenCode, Cowork, Zia Tutor AI, MCP, Postgres, pgvector, Agent SDK
- Certification codes: PCAO-F, PCAR-F, PCDV-F, PCAR-P, CCAO-F, CCAR-F, and the "FDE Internship Program"
- Everyday technical words learners already say in English: prompt, agent, model, token, context window, workflow, deploy

The reason: learners will meet these terms in the book, in the exams and in job descriptions. A clever Urdu coinage they never see again makes the content harder to use, not easier.

In Urdu script, it's fine to write English terms in Latin script inside the Urdu sentence, or to transliterate common ones (for example, پرامپٹ for prompt). Be consistent within a piece.

## Translation, adaptation, or a separate piece?

Label every multi-language item in the plan with one of these:

- **Translation**: same piece, same structure, faithful wording. Use it for facts that must not drift: exam rules, dates, announcements.
- **Adaptation**: same core idea, rewritten for the audience. Local examples (a Lahore clinic, a Karachi logistics firm, freelancing on Upwork/Fiverr), a different hook, a different length. This is the default for educational and shareable content.
- **Separate piece**: the topic only makes sense for one audience. Examples: "Freelancing as an FDE from Pakistan", or a Pakistan-specific career Q&A, which has no English twin.

Translating an English thought-leadership post word for word usually produces something stiff that performs worse than either a native Urdu piece or the English original. Prefer adaptation.

## Hooks and tone

- Urdu and Roman Urdu content can be warmer and more direct ("Aap ka kaam AI kar sakta hai? Ye dekhein…") than the professional English register on LinkedIn.
- Career and money questions are what this audience cares about most. Lead with them, and keep the claims honest (see `claims.md`). Don't promise jobs or income.
- For beginners, open with a familiar task (WhatsApp replies for a shop, invoice summaries, school admin) before naming the concept.

## Worked examples

**Same idea, three versions. Source: the Mode 2 hub (/docs/mode-2-manufacturing), "labour as a task vs labour as an asset".**

- *English (LinkedIn)*: "Every hour you spend repeating a task is labour spent. A Digital FTE turns that task into an asset: built once, working while you sleep. Mode 1 solves it once. Mode 2 builds the worker. [link]"
- *Roman Urdu (Instagram caption)*: "Jo kaam aap roz baar baar karte hain, woh 'task' hai. Usko ek baar Digital FTE bana dein, phir woh 'asset' ban jata hai, jo aap ke sone ke waqt bhi kaam karta hai. Mode 1 vs Mode 2, link bio mein."
- *Urdu script (video subtitle / long explainer opening)*: "جو کام آپ ہر روز دہراتے ہیں، وہ محنت ہے جو ہر بار خرچ ہوتی ہے۔ Digital FTE اسی کام کو ایک اثاثہ بنا دیتا ہے۔"

These are illustrations of register, not approved copy. Have a fluent Urdu writer on the team review anything in Urdu script before it's published.
