## 1. Goal, audience and language mix

**Goal: thought leadership.** By the end of January, people outside Panaversity should be using the book's terms in their own posts and comments and linking to the book when they do. The terms are "the right side of the line", "Digital FTE", "the agent is the operating layer", and "labour as a task vs. labour as an asset". People arguing with the ideas counts as success too. A post that collects 40 thoughtful disagreements does more for the goal than one that collects 400 likes. Each piece has one primary next step: **read the source page in the book** (usually the Thesis, the Preface or The Operating Layer). The secondary step, used on fewer pieces, is **subscribe to the page's newsletter**, which becomes the owned list for this audience.

**Audience.** A global English-reading professional audience, most of it at the *Stranger* stage of the journey:
- **Enterprise executives and department leaders** (the primary target). They decide whether "Digital FTE" enters their organisation's vocabulary. Entry pages: Thesis, The Operating Layer, AI Is Non-Negotiable.
- **Product managers, architects and senior engineers.** They argue about the technical claims (the operating layer, the reasoning engine as replaceable, governance as the moat). Entry page: The Operating Layer.
- **Consultants and services professionals.** The repricing story is about their industry. Entry pages: Preface, The Roles This Book Trains.

**Language.** All pieces are **English**, per the bilingual playbook (LinkedIn thought leadership is written in English for an international professional audience). Book terms are written exactly as the book writes them. Pakistan-facing Urdu or Roman Urdu versions are **out of scope** for this page. Where a piece adapts well (for example the "task vs. asset" post), section 5 marks it as a candidate for a later **adaptation** on Panaversity's Instagram and Facebook. Those would be adaptations, not translations.

**Assumptions** (no one answered the section 9 questions, so these are stated rather than asked):
1. **Capacity:** one writer or editor, about 0.5 of a designer, and a few hours a month of Zia Khan's time for reviewing posts, recording one short video a month and hosting one LinkedIn Live a month. If capacity is lower, drop the experimental slots first, then the second weekly post.
2. **Personal profiles carry the reach.** Company pages usually get less organic reach than people do. The page publishes the canonical post. Zia Khan and the co-authors (Wania Kazmi, Muhammad Junaid, Rehan ul Haq) then reshare it with their own one-paragraph take, or post first and have the page reshare. This is a working assumption, not a measured fact. Check it against the page's analytics in weeks 1 and 2.
3. **The page can publish a LinkedIn newsletter.** [verify] the page has the newsletter feature turned on and who holds admin rights to it.
4. **No baseline numbers were shared**, so the targets in section 4 are relative to a baseline set in weeks 1 and 2, not invented absolute figures.
5. **Holiday dip.** Late December engagement is usually lower for a global professional audience, so weeks 8 and 9 run a lighter cadence.
6. **No launch is tied to this quarter.** If a new crash course, exam window or cohort lands in the quarter, add it as a single "the argument in practice" post. Don't turn this campaign into a funnel.

## 2. Pillars in play

The plan draws mainly on **Pillar 1, The Line**. The book calls it the thought-leadership engine. Two supporting pillars give the argument proof and consequences. The front matter itself names three arguments: architectural (Thesis), interface (The Operating Layer) and ownership (What You Carry In). The quarter is built around those three, one per month, opened by the Preface's "line".

For example: "value is shifting from those who use software to those who own the agents that run on top of it", "SaaS dies as a destination because the agent replaces the app. The PC as we know it dies because the agent replaces *you* at the controls", and "The capability is real. That gives us ready-made material for debate posts that don't look like self-promotion.

It is also the natural bridge for readers who want to act. This quarter it stays an argument, not a certification pitch.
