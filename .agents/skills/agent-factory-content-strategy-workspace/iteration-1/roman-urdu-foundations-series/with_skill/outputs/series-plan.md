# Series Plan: "AI Seekhein, Zero Se" (Foundations Crash Courses)

**Instagram Reels + YouTube Shorts · Roman Urdu · 3 posts/week · 4 weeks (12 posts)**
Prepared 28 October 2026. Course names, order and ideas were checked against the Agent Factory System of Record (corpus generation 82) and the live Foundations hub: https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/foundations

---

## 1. Goal, audience, language (with assumptions)

- **Goal (assumed): Readers.** Get Pakistani beginners to open the Foundations hub and start the first crash course, and keep going in order. **Community** (follow/subscribe) is the secondary goal. Each post has **one** primary CTA.
- **Audience:** Students, fresh graduates, freelancers and career-changers in Pakistan (the PIAIC audience). They have no coding background and mostly use AI from their phones. The hub itself says *"If you can use a website, you can do all ten"*, and that line is the series promise.
- **Language:** Spoken Roman Urdu / Urdu. On-screen text and captions are in **Roman Urdu**. Book terms stay **in English**: prompt, token, context window, Skill, connector, Digital FTE, Markdown, HTML, workflow. Each piece is an **adaptation** of a book course with Pakistani examples. These are not translations. An English version is not planned for this series.
- **Journey stage:** Stranger → Reader. All CTAs point to a crash course page or the hub. Zia Tutor appears once, in Week 4, because its setup (adding a custom connector in claude.ai) is a step too far for a first contact.
- **Assumed capacity:** One presenter who speaks Roman Urdu and one editor. Each post is **one 30–60 second vertical video**, published as an IG Reel and a YT Short on the same day. Only the caption and CTA wording change per platform.
- **Assumed schedule:** **Mon / Wed / Fri**, starting Monday 2 November 2026. Move the dates if the team has a launch or cohort to align with.
- **Series rule:** Every post covers **one course and one idea**, in the book's order. The series doubles as a guided route through Foundations.

## 2. Pillar in play

**Pillar 2: AI Fluency for Everyone.** Hub: https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/foundations

The ten Foundations courses are in this order (confirmed via `outline_agent_factory`): Just Delegate It, What AI Actually Is, AI Fluency (4Ds), AI Prompting in 2026, Markdown In HTML Out, Code You Never Write, Skills & Connectors, How to Think in the AI Era, Workflow Design & Diagnosis, Governance Risk & Responsible Use. The hub says they "build on each other". Each course gets one post. The other two slots are a series trailer (post 1) and a recap with the next step (post 12).

Split: **~25% searchable** (posts 3, 5, 7: concept explainers that people search on YouTube), **~65% shareable** (hooks, myth-busting, relatable stories), **~10% experimental** (post 12: a comment-driven format). The split leans toward shareable on purpose, because Reels and Shorts are discovery channels.

## 4. Post-by-post: hook, topic, CTA

On-screen text and spoken lines are in Roman Urdu. "Beats" are the 3–4 points the video covers in 30–60 seconds.

### Week 1: "AI ko kaam dena seekhein"

**Post 1: Series trailer** (Mon 2 Nov)
- **Hook (first 2 sec):** "Har koi kehta hai 'AI seekho'. Lekin kahan se shuru karein? Ye raha jawab."
- **Topic:** The Foundations path. Ten crash courses in order, for everyone, with no coding. Source line: *"If you can use a website, you can do all ten."*
- **Beats:** (1) The problem: random YouTube tutorials, no order. (2) The book: *The AI Agent Factory*, Foundations, 10 courses that build on each other. (3) The series: 4 weeks, Mon/Wed/Fri, one course per post.
- **CTA:** "Follow karein. Agla video Wednesday ko: course #1."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/foundations

**Post 2: Just Delegate It** (Wed 4 Nov)
- **Hook:** "Aap AI se sirf sawal poochte hain? Isi liye kaam ab bhi aap hi kar rahe hain."
- **Topic:** The course's opening idea. Most people *ask* AI things and the work stays with them. This course has you **hand AI a job**, watch it work, check the result, and decide whether to accept it. The Delegation Loop: Define → Delegate → Observe → Intervene → Verify → Accept. *"AI owns the middle. You own both ends."*
- **Pakistani example (adaptation):** "Meri dukaan ke liye 5 WhatsApp reply bana do" versus a job brief that starts "When this is done, I will have…" (the course's own exercise).
- **CTA:** "Course #1 free account aur browser se ho jata hai. Link bio mein, aaj hi pehla 'job' dein."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/just-delegate-it-crash-course

**Post 3: What AI Actually Is (1/2)** (Fri 6 Nov) *[searchable]*
- **Hook:** "AI Google nahi hai. Ye kuch dhoondta hi nahi."
- **Topic:** Idea 1 of the course. A language model **predicts the next piece of text** (tokens). It never looks facts up. It works like the world's best-read autocomplete, not a librarian. That is why it is strong on common facts and makes things up on rare ones.
- **Beats:** Autocomplete "Happy birthday to…" → "you". The capital of France works. The plot of an unknown local book gets a confident made-up answer.
- **CTA:** "Ye course pehle parhein, baaki sab aasan ho jata hai. Course #2, link bio mein."
- **Title for YT search:** "AI kaise kaam karta hai? (Roman Urdu) | What AI Actually Is"
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/what-ai-actually-is-crash-course

### Week 2: "AI ko samjhein, phir istemal karein"

**Post 4: What AI Actually Is (2/2)** (Mon 9 Nov)
- **Hook:** "AI ne itne confidence se ghalat jawab diya ke aap ne maan liya? Ye wajah hai."
- **Topic:** Ideas 3 and 6. The model has no separate truth-checker, so **hallucination is the machine working as built**. Its confident, agreeable tone is a learned *style*, not proof. *"The certain tone is a manner, not a verdict."*
- **Pakistani example:** A student asks AI for a past-paper fact or a university deadline. The answer sounds sure, and it is wrong.
- **CTA:** "Save karein, aur course #2 ke baqi 7 ideas link bio mein."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/what-ai-actually-is-crash-course

**Post 5: AI Fluency, the 4Ds** (Wed 11 Nov) *[searchable]*
- **Hook:** "Same AI, same subah, do log. Ek ka kaam ban gaya, doosre ka kachra. Farq tool ka nahi tha."
- **Topic:** AI access is not AI fluency. The **4Ds**: **Delegation** (decide), **Description** (explain), **Discernment** (check), **Diligence** (own). Attribute the framework: it was created by Prof. Rick Dakan and Prof. Joseph Feller in courses produced with Anthropic, as the course states.
- **Beats:** One card per D, using the course's beginner checklist questions in Roman Urdu.
- **CTA:** "4D checklist ka screenshot lein. Poora course #3 link bio mein."
- **Title for YT search:** "AI Fluency kya hai? 4D Framework Roman Urdu mein"
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/ai-fluency-crash-course

**Post 6: AI Prompting in 2026** (Fri 13 Nov)
- **Hook:** "'Magic prompts' wali reels band karein. Asal cheez prompt nahi, context hai."
- **Topic:** The course's one-sentence takeaway: **"get the right context in, keep the wrong context out."** Same model, same question, different answer, because the context changed. The course's example: a bare "physics vs zoology" question versus the same question with your results and your schedule attached.
- **Pakistani example (adaptation):** "Konsa degree karun?" with nothing attached, versus the same question with your Matric/FSc marks and interests.
- **CTA:** "Course #4 parhein. Is ek line se aap zyada-tar users se aage hain. Link bio mein."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/ai-prompting-2026

### Week 3: "AI se asli kaam karwaein"

**Post 7: Markdown In, HTML Out** (Mon 16 Nov) *[searchable]*
- **Hook:** "AI ko likhne ki ek zubaan hai, aur parhne ki doosri. Dono 1 ghante se kam mein."
- **Topic:** Write **to** AI in **Markdown** (`#` for a heading, `-` for a bullet), so it doesn't have to guess. Ask for **HTML** back when a human will read a long answer. Neither one is programming. The deciding question: **"Who reads this last?"**
- **Beats:** A screen recording of a messy prompt next to the same prompt with Markdown headings, then "ask for an HTML page" to get a readable result.
- **CTA:** "Course #5 link bio mein. Aaj ka prompt Markdown mein likh kar dekhein."
- **Note:** Don't quote "1 ghanta" as a promise. The course says Markdown "takes an hour to learn", so attribute it: "course kehta hai…".
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/markdown-html-crash-course

**Post 8: Code You Never Write** (Wed 18 Nov)
- **Hook:** "Coding nahi aati? Phir bhi AI se code likhwa kar 1,400 rows check ho sakti hain."
- **Topic:** You describe the problem in plain words, the AI writes and runs the code, and **you check what it did**. The book's bookkeeper story: two files that should match, 1,400 rows reconciled, 23 mismatches flagged. Also the course's warning case: AI "read" a school fee sheet and named 8 families in arrears when the real number was 11. That is why numbers should come from code that ran.
- **Pakistani example (adaptation):** A school admin with a fee spreadsheet, or a freelancer with 300 badly named client files.
- **CTA:** "Course #6, 'even if you have never coded'. Link bio mein."
- **Claims note:** Present the bookkeeper and fee-sheet cases as the book's examples ("book mein ek bookkeeper ki misaal hai…"), not as Panaversity learner results.
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/code-you-never-write-crash-course

**Post 9: Skills & Connectors** (Fri 20 Nov)
- **Hook:** "Har baar AI ko wohi baat dobara samjhate hain? Ek baar sikhaein, bas."
- **Topic:** A **Skill** is saved instructions (a SKILL.md file) that teach AI a repeatable task your way. A **connector** is a safe link to an app such as Google Drive or Gmail. *"Connectors are the kitchen, Skills are the recipe cards."* Safety habit: start read-only and keep the scope small.
- **Pakistani example (adaptation):** A freelancer's "client proposal in my style" Skill. A connector to the Drive folder that holds past proposals.
- **CTA:** "Course #7 link bio mein. Jo Skill aap banate hain, woh har tool mein saath chalti hai."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/skills-connectors-crash-course

### Week 4: "AI ke saath sochna, aur safe rehna"

**Post 10: How to Think in the AI Era** (Mon 23 Nov)
- **Hook:** "AI ka pehla jawab aap ki raye ban jata hai? Ye sab se khatarnak aadat hai."
- **Topic:** The course's contrast: *Person A formed her own opinion before asking AI. Person B let AI's first answer become her opinion.* "AI gravity" pulls you toward the first answer. Use AI to stretch a skill you already have, not to skip learning it. Before you ask, write down your own decision and what would change your mind (the "Prediction Lock").
- **Pakistani example:** A student choosing a university, or a freelancer pricing a gig.
- **CTA:** "Course #8 link bio mein. Ye woh judgment hai jo baqi sab courses ke baad bhi kaam aata hai."
- **Source:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/how-to-think-ai-era

**Post 11: Workflow Design + Governance** (Wed 25 Nov)
- **Hook:** "Customer ki list AI mein paste karne se pehle 5 second ruk jaein."
- **Topic:** Governance course: **"Classify before you act."** Red, yellow and green data. Passwords and credentials are always red. Customer names and account numbers need a check first. Public material is generally fine in an approved tool. Bridge from Workflow Design: *"The output is not the verdict."* A good draft doesn't prove a step is safe to hand over.
- **Story (from the course):** A project manager uploads a customer spreadsheet to answer her boss before lunch. "She wasn't trying to break a rule. That's how most incidents happen."
- **CTA:** "Course #10 (aur #9) link bio mein. Kisi aise dost ko bhejein jo office ka data AI mein daalta hai."
- **Sources:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/governance-risk-responsible-use-crash-course · https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/workflow-design-diagnosis-crash-course

**Post 12: Recap + "Aap ka sawal"** (Fri 27 Nov) *[experimental]*
- **Hook:** "4 hafte, 10 courses, 1 line har course ki. Kitne yaad hain?"
- **Topic:** A fast recap with one line per course (reuse each post's key line), then the next step: **General Agents** is the next section after Foundations. Introduce **Zia Tutor AI**, the book's personal tutor, as the way to keep learning.
- **Experiment:** Ask viewers to comment their biggest beginner question. The best questions become "Ask Zia" answers in the next series. This tests whether comment-led Shorts grow engagement.
- **CTA (one):** "Comment mein apna sab se bara AI sawal likhein. Agli series inhi ke jawab hogi."
- **Sources:** https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/foundations · https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/ecosystem/zia-tutor-ai · https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/general-agents

## 5. Distribution

- **Owned:** The link in bio and the YT description go to the **Foundations hub**, not a homepage. Share each post in the PIAIC/Panaversity WhatsApp or community groups in Roman Urdu, with the course link. At the end of Week 4, send the community a message listing all 12 videos plus the hub, as a "Foundations route".
- **Rented:** The same vertical file goes to IG Reels and YT Shorts on the same day. Add a YT Shorts playlist, "AI Seekhein Zero Se", so viewers watch the whole series in order. Facebook Reels can reuse the file at no extra cost.
- **Borrowed:** Ask PIAIC instructors or learner ambassadors to duet/remix posts 2, 8 and 11, which have the most relatable stories.
- **Atomise:** Posts 5 (4Ds) and 11 (red/yellow/green data) also work as IG carousels, a saveable checklist format. Reuse the Week 4 recap as a pinned post.
- **Measure:** Saves and shares per post (shareable posts), hub link clicks from bio and description (the readers goal), and comments on post 12 (the experiment). After Week 2, move the strongest hook style into Weeks 3–4.
