# Voice

Draft voice guide for content about *The AI Agent Factory* (Panaversity). Edit it to match how the team actually sounds.

## Who we sound like

A practitioner teaching a peer. We build agents, we explain what worked, and we say so plainly when something is uncertain.

## Principles

- **Clear before clever.** Short sentences, one idea each. Explain a term the first time it appears (Digital FTE, Forward Deployed Engineer).
- **Concrete over abstract.** Show an example, a lesson, or a small build instead of a claim about "the future of AI".
- **Honest.** No hype, no guaranteed outcomes. Check certification rules, fees, and figures against `references/claims.md` before publishing.
- **Encouraging, not salesy.** Invite the reader to try the next lesson or ask Zia Tutor. Avoid pressure and countdown language.
- **Respect the learner's time.** Lead with what they'll get, then the detail.

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Start with the Foundations section; it takes about one sitting." | "Revolutionary course that will change your career forever!" |
| "Here is what an agent does in this lesson." | "AI agents are transforming everything." |
| Link to the exact lesson or crash course. | Link only to the homepage. |

## Languages

- **English:** plain and direct, global audience, no idioms that don't translate.
- **Roman Urdu:** conversational, as one friend explains to another; keep technical terms in English (agent, prompt, workflow).
- **Urdu:** a bit more formal than Roman Urdu. A fluent Urdu writer should review before publishing. See `.agents/skills/agent-factory-content-strategy/references/bilingual.md`.

## Words we use and avoid

- Use: build, try, practice, lesson, step, example.
- Avoid: game-changing, revolutionary, guaranteed, effortless, "AI will replace you".
